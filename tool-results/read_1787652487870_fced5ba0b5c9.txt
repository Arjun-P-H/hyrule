     1→# 1. Executive overview of the proposed mathematical world
     2→
     3→The proposed system abandons conventional polygon-based rendering in favor of a **purely mathematical, continuous scalar field** evaluated in real-time via GPU raymarching. The world fundamentally exists as a 2D plane $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, governed by a deterministic master function $W(x,y,\text{seed})$. This function outputs environmental properties (elevation, moisture, temperature, biome weights). 
     4→
     5→To achieve the visual illusion of a 3D natural world, the 2D elevation field $E(x,y)$ is treated as a heightmap in a pseudo-3D space. A virtual camera casts mathematical rays through a 2D screen projection into this space, intersecting the continuous surface $z = E(x,y)$. Lighting, atmosphere, and depth are derived analytically from the gradients and intersection distances of this field. The result is an infinitely extensible, seamlessly coherent, and entirely asset-free natural environment where every visual pixel is a direct evaluation of underlying mathematics.
     6→
     7→# 2. Fundamental mathematical assumptions
     8→
     9→1. **Continuity**: The world is defined over $\mathbb{R}^2$. There are no discrete grids or voxels at the foundational level; discretization only occurs at the screen-sample (pixel) level.
    10→2. **Determinism**: $W(x,y,\text{seed})$ is a pure function. It has no side effects and relies on no external state.
    11→3. **Spatial Coherence**: The fields are Lipschitz continuous or smoothly differentiable almost everywhere, ensuring no visual tearing or infinite gradients.
    12→4. **Multi-scale Self-Similarity**: The world exhibits fractal properties, where statistical characteristics repeat across spatial frequencies.
    13→5. **Computational Locality**: The state at $(x,y)$ can be computed using only local mathematical operations and a bounded neighborhood, enabling massive parallelization on a GPU.
    14→
    15→# 3. Coordinate system
    16→
    17→The world utilizes a dual-coordinate architecture to balance infinite scale with numerical precision:
    18→* **Global Coordinates $(X, Y)$**: Represents the absolute position in the infinite world. Stored as 64-bit floating-point (`double`) or a pair of 32-bit floats (double-float emulation) on the CPU to prevent precision loss at extreme distances.
    19→* **Local Coordinates $(x, y)$**: Represents the position relative to the current camera chunk. $x = X - X_{\text{chunk}}$, $y = Y - Y_{\text{chunk}}$. Stored as 32-bit floating-point (`float`) in the GPU shader. All mathematical field evaluations use $(x,y)$ to ensure high precision and avoid floating-point jitter.
    20→
    21→# 4. Seed and deterministic hashing
    22→
    23→To ensure determinism without storing state, we use a 32-bit integer hash function based on the PCG/Murmur3 finalizer. This maps integer coordinates and a seed to a pseudo-random uniform distribution.
    24→
    25→$$ H(x_i, y_i, s) = \text{finalize}(x_i \oplus (y_i \times 0x27D4EB2F) \oplus (s \times 0x9E3779B9)) $$
    26→
    27→Where $\text{finalize}(h)$ applies bitwise shifts and multiplications to avalanche the bits:
    28→$$ h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 16; \quad h \times= 0x85EBCA6B; \quad h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 13; \quad h \times= 0xC2B2AE35; \quad h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 16 $$
    29→
    30→To generate continuous noise, we evaluate $H$ at the integer lattice points $\lfloor x \rfloor, \lfloor y \rfloor$, convert to floats $U = H / 4294967295.0 \in [0,1]$, and interpolate using a quintic smoothstep $S(t) = 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3$ to ensure $C^2$ continuity (continuous second derivatives), eliminating visible grid artifacts.
    31→
    32→# 5. Macro terrain equation
    33→
    34→The macro terrain defines continents, oceans, and major tectonic plates. It uses low-frequency Fractal Brownian Motion (fBm).
    35→
    36→$$ E_{\text{macro}}(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_{\text{macro}}-1} a_i N(f_i x, f_i y) $$
    37→
    38→* **Variables**: $N$ is the base continuous noise function $\in [-1, 1]$. $f_i = f_0 \cdot L^i$ is the frequency (lacunarity $L \approx 2.0$). $a_i = P^i$ is the amplitude (persistence $P \approx 0.5$).
    39→* **Range**: Approximately $[-1.5, 1.5]$, normalized to $[-1, 1]$.
    40→* **Utility**: Creates broad, sweeping landmasses. Values $< 0$ conceptually represent ocean basins; values $> 0$ represent land.
    41→* **Interaction**: Acts as a base mask and elevation offset for all meso and micro scales.
    42→
    43→# 6. Mountain equation
    44→
    45→Mountains require sharp ridges and asymmetric slopes, which standard fBm cannot produce. We use a **Ridged Multifractal** modulated by domain warping.
    46→
    47→First, the ridge function:
    48→$$ R(x,y) = 1.0 - |N(x,y)| $$
    49→To sharpen the peaks, we square it: $R_{\text{sharp}} = R(x,y)^2$.
    50→
    51→The mountain elevation field:
    52→$$ E_{\text{mount}}(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_{\text{mount}}-1} a_i \left( 1.0 - |N(f_i x + W_x, f_i y + W_y)| \right)^2 \cdot M_{\text{macro}}(x,y) $$
    53→
    54→* **Variables**: $W_x, W_y$ are domain warp offsets (see Sec 14). $M_{\text{macro}}$ is a mask derived from $E_{\text{macro}}$ ensuring mountains only form on land.
    55→* **Range**: $[0, \text{MaxHeight}]$.
    56→* **Utility**: The absolute value creates sharp discontinuities in the derivative (ridges). Squaring narrows the peaks. The mask prevents mountains from forming in the ocean.
    57→* **Parameters**: Higher persistence ($P \approx 0.6$) creates more jagged, alpine peaks.
    58→
    59→# 7. Valley equation
    60→
    61→Valleys and canyons are carved using **Cellular (Worley) Noise**, which calculates the distance to the nearest randomly distributed seed point.
    62→
    63→Let $F_1(x,y)$ be the distance to the nearest seed, and $F_2(x,y)$ be the distance to the second nearest. The distance to the cell boundary (the "edge") is:
    64→$$ D_{\text{edge}}(x,y) = F_2(x,y) - F_1(x,y) $$
    65→
    66→The valley carving function:
    67→$$ E_{\text{valley}}(x,y) = C_{\text{depth}} \cdot \left( 1.0 - \text{smoothstep}(0, W_{\text{valley}}, D_{\text{edge}}(x,y)) \right)^2 $$
    68→
    69→* **Variables**: $C_{\text{depth}}$ is maximum carving depth. $W_{\text{valley}}$ controls valley width.
    70→* **Range**: $[0, C_{\text{depth}}]$.
    71→* **Utility**: $D_{\text{edge}}$ is exactly $0$ at the boundaries between Voronoi cells. This creates a natural, branching network of low points that perfectly mimics fluvial erosion and drainage basins.
    72→* **Interaction**: Subtracted from the combined macro and mountain elevation.
    73→
    74→# 8. Water equation
    75→
    76→Water is not an independent layer; it is a mathematical consequence of elevation and cellular boundaries.
    77→
    78→**Oceans and Lakes**:
    79→$$ W_{\text{body}}(x,y) = \text{smoothstep}(E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) - \epsilon, E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) + \epsilon, W_{\text{level}}) $$
    80→Where $E_{\text{terrain}} = E_{\text{macro}} + E_{\text{mount}} - E_{\text{valley}}$.
    81→
    82→**Rivers and Streams**:
    83→Rivers flow along the Voronoi edges defined in the valley equation, but only where the terrain is above sea level and slope is sufficient.
    84→$$ W_{\text{river}}(x,y) = \text{smoothstep}(W_{\text{river\_width}}, 0, D_{\text{edge}}(x,y)) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(W_{\text{level}}, W_{\text{level}} + 0.1, E_{\text{terrain}}) \cdot \|\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}\| $$
    85→
    86→* **Utility**: This guarantees rivers naturally connect lakes, follow the steepest descent (via the gradient magnitude multiplier), and never flow uphill or exist in the ocean.
    87→
    88→# 9. Climate equations
    89→
    90→Climate is derived from global position, elevation, and noise, avoiding disconnected random maps.
    91→
    92→**Temperature**:
    93→$$ T(x,y) = T_{\text{base}} - \lambda_{\text{lat}} |Y_{\text{global}}| - \lambda_{\text{alt}} \max(0, E_{\text{terrain}}) + N_{\text{temp}}(x,y) $$
    94→* **Variables**: $\lambda_{\text{lat}}$ is the latitude cooling rate. $\lambda_{\text{alt}}$ is the adiabatic lapse rate (altitude cooling). $N_{\text{temp}}$ is low-frequency noise for local climate anomalies.
    95→* **Range**: $[-1, 1]$ (normalized, where $-1$ is freezing, $1$ is hot).
    96→
    97→**Moisture**:
    98→$$ M(x,y) = M_{\text{base}} + N_{\text{moist}}(x,y) + \lambda_{\text{ocean}} \exp(-D_{\text{ocean}}(x,y) / \sigma_{\text{moist}}) $$
    99→* **Variables**: $D_{\text{ocean}}$ is the distance to the nearest ocean (approximated via the macro field). $\sigma_{\text{moist}}$ is the moisture decay distance.
   100→* **Range**: $[0, 1]$.
   101→
   102→# 10. Biome equation
   103→
   104→Biomes are calculated using continuous radial basis functions (RBF) in the $(T, M, E)$ space, ensuring perfectly smooth transitions without hard thresholds.
   105→
   106→Define $K$ biome centers $C_k = (T_k, M_k, E_k)$. The weight for biome $k$ is:
   107→$$ w_k(x,y) = \exp\left( - \left( \frac{(T - T_k)^2}{\sigma_{T,k}^2} + \frac{(M - M_k)^2}{\sigma_{M,k}^2} + \frac{(E - E_k)^2}{\sigma_{E,k}^2} \right) \right) $$
   108→
   109→Normalize the weights:
   110→$$ W_k(x,y) = \frac{w_k(x,y)}{\sum_{j=1}^K w_j(x,y)} $$
   111→
   112→* **Utility**: Any environmental property $P$ (color, vegetation density, roughness) is computed as $P(x,y) = \sum_{k=1}^K W_k(x,y) P_k$. This guarantees $C^\infty$ smooth blending between biomes.
   113→
   114→# 11. Vegetation equation
   115→
   116→Vegetation is a scalar density field $V(x,y)$ that modulates surface color and perturbs the surface normal to simulate grass/leaves without 3D models.
   117→
   118→$$ V(x,y) = \left( \sum_{k=1}^K W_k(x,y) V_{\text{base},k} \right) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(0.2, 0.4, M(x,y)) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(0.8, 0.6, \|\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}\|) $$
   119→
   120→* **Variables**: $V_{\text{base},k}$ is the max vegetation for biome $k$. The smoothsteps ensure plants only grow with sufficient moisture and on flat ground (not cliffs).
   121→* **Visual Application**: $V(x,y)$ is used to mix rock/dirt colors with green colors, and added as high-frequency noise to the surface normal $\vec{n}$ to create a fuzzy, grass-like shading effect.
   122→
   123→# 12. Geological anomaly equation
   124→
   125→To create rare, unusual mathematical structures (e.g., spiral valleys, fractal craters), we use a low-frequency Voronoi grid to assign unique IDs to massive regions.
   126→
   127→$$ \text{CellID} = \text{WorleyID}(X \cdot S_{\text{anom}}, Y \cdot S_{\text{anom}}) $$
   128→$$ \text{AnomHash} = H(\text{CellID}_x, \text{CellID}_y, \text{seed}) / 4294967295.0 $$
   129→
   130→If $\text{AnomHash} < P_{\text{anom}}$ (e.g., 0.005 for 0.5% rarity):
   131→Calculate distance to cell center $D_c$. Apply a radial falloff $F = \text{smoothstep}(R_{\text{anom}}, 0, D_c)$.
   132→Generate anomalous terrain $E_{\text{anom}}$ using non-standard math (e.g., $E_{\text{anom}} = \sin(D_c \cdot 10) \cdot \cos(\theta \cdot 5)$).
   133→$$ E_{\text{final}} = \text{mix}(E_{\text{terrain}}, E_{\text{anom}}, F) $$
   134→
   135→* **Utility**: Guarantees statistical rarity, absolute determinism, and spatial coherence without storing a database of landmarks.
   136→
   137→# 13. Regional mathematical-rule equation
   138→
   139→Large regions are governed by different terrain generation algorithms. We use a macro Voronoi grid (scale $S_{\text{region}} \approx 0.0001$).
   140→
   141→Let $R_{\text{id}}$ be the region ID. We define different terrain functions:
   142→* $f_0$: Standard fBm (Mountains)
   143→* $f_1$: Trigonometric waves (Rolling dunes/hills)
   144→* $f_2$: Quantized/Stepped noise (Mesas/Plateaus)
   145→
   146→The blending weight between the current region and its neighbor is derived from the Worley edge distance $D_{\text{edge\_region}}$:
   147→$$ B_{\text{region}} = \text{smoothstep}(0, W_{\text{blend}}, D_{\text{edge\_region}}) $$
   148→The final terrain smoothly interpolates between the mathematical rules of adjacent regions.
   149→
   150→# 14. Domain-warping system
   151→
   152→Domain warping breaks the isotropic (directionally uniform) look of standard noise, creating organic, fluid-like geological folding.
   153→
   154→$$ q(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \text{fBm}(x, y) \\ \text{fBm}(x + 5.2, y + 1.3) \end{pmatrix} $$
   155→$$ r(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \text{fBm}(x + 4.0 q_x, y + 4.0 q_y) \\ \text{fBm}(x + 4.0 q_x + 1.7, y + 4.0 q_y + 9.2) \end{pmatrix} $$
   156→$$ \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + S_{\text{warp}} \cdot r(x,y) $$
   157→
   158→* **Variables**: $S_{\text{warp}}$ is the warp strength.
   159→* **Utility**: The nested fBm evaluations create chaotic, swirling distortions. When $E(x', y')$ is evaluated, it produces winding mountain ranges, meandering coastlines, and highly natural, non-repetitive structures.
   160→
   161→# 15. Pseudo-3D projection model
   162→
   163→I investigated five models. **Model E (Ray-like mathematical sampling / Heightfield Raymarching)** is the superior choice.
   164→
   165→* **Model A (Orthographic)**: Lacks depth perception.
   166→* **Model B (Perspective-warped 2D)**: Creates "treadmilling" artifacts and fails at steep angles.
   167→* **Model C (Height-dependent)**: Complex to implement, breaks at horizons.
   168→* **Model D (Layered depth)**: Looks like cardboard cutouts (parallax scrolling).
   169→* **Model E (Raymarching)**: The 2D field $E(x,y)$ is treated as a surface $z = E(x,y)$ in 3D space. Rays are cast from the camera. This natively produces true perspective, occlusion, parallax, and shadows from a purely 2D mathematical field.
   170→
   171→**Recommendation**: Model E. It perfectly fulfills the "2D world pretending to be 3D" requirement while providing genuine 3D visual fidelity.
   172→
   173→# 16. Depth model
   174→
   175→Depth is the distance $t$ along the view ray $\vec{R}$ from the camera origin $\vec{C}$ to the intersection point $\vec{P}$ where $P_z = E(P_x, P_y)$.
   176→
   177→$$ \vec{P}(t) = \vec{C} + t \vec{R} $$
   178→The raymarching loop solves for $t$ iteratively:
   179→$$ t_{i+1} = t_i + \max(\Delta_{\text{min}}, (P_{z,i} - E(P_{x,i}, P_{y,i})) \cdot \lambda_{\text{step}}) $$
   180→Where $\lambda_{\text{step}} \approx 0.5$ ensures we don't overstep the surface. Depth $t$ is used for fog, atmospheric scattering, and level-of-detail (LOD) noise cutoff.
   181→
   182→# 17. Surface-normal model
   183→
   184→The surface normal $\vec{n}$ is derived analytically from the elevation gradient $\nabla E$.
   185→
   186→$$ \nabla E(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial E}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial E}{\partial y} \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} \frac{E(x+\epsilon, y) - E(x-\epsilon, y)}{2\epsilon} \\ \frac{E(x, y+\epsilon) - E(x, y-\epsilon)}{2\epsilon} \end{pmatrix} $$
   187→
   188→The 3D normal vector is:
   189→$$ \vec{n}(x,y) = \frac{(-\frac{\partial E}{\partial x}, -\frac{\partial E}{\partial y}, 1)}{\sqrt{(\frac{\partial E}{\partial x})^2 + (\frac{\partial E}{\partial y})^2 + 1}} $$
   190→* **Optimization**: $\epsilon$ is scaled by the ray distance $t$ to maintain constant screen-space detail and avoid aliasing.
   191→
   192→# 18. Lighting model
   193→
   194→Lighting uses a Lambertian diffuse model with a Blinn-Phong specular highlight for water, plus shadow raymarching.
   195→
   196→$$ L_{\text{diffuse}} = \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) $$
   197→$$ L_{\text{specular}} = \left( \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{H}) \right)^{\alpha} \cdot W_{\text{body}} $$
   198→Where $\vec{H} = \text{normalize}(\vec{L}_{\text{sun}} - \vec{R})$.
   199→
   200→**Shadows**: To check if a point $\vec{P}$ is in shadow, march a secondary ray from $\vec{P}$ toward $\vec{L}_{\text{sun}}$. If $P_z(t) < E(P_x(t), P_y(t))$ at any point before reaching the sky, $L_{\text{shadow}} = 0$, else $1$.
   201→
   202→# 19. Atmospheric model
   203→
   204→Atmosphere is modeled using the Beer-Lambert law for light extinction and in-scattering.
   205→
   206→$$ \tau(t) = \int_0^t \sigma(s) ds \approx \sigma_{\text{fog}} \cdot t $$
   207→$$ T_{\text{transmittance}} = \exp(-\tau(t)) $$
   208→$$ I_{\text{inscatter}} = C_{\text{sky}} \cdot (1 - \exp(-\tau(t))) $$
   209→
   210→* **Variables**: $\sigma_{\text{fog}}$ is the scattering coefficient (density). $C_{\text{sky}}$ is the sky color at the ray's altitude.
   211→* **Utility**: Distant objects smoothly fade into the sky color, providing massive depth cues and hiding the raymarching cutoff distance.
   212→
   213→# 20. Sky model
   214→
   215→The sky is a procedural gradient with raymarched volumetric clouds.
   216→
   217→**Gradient**:
   218→$$ C_{\text{sky}}(\vec{R}) = \text{mix}(C_{\text{horizon}}, C_{\text{zenith}}, \max(0, R_z)^{0.5}) $$
   219→
   220→**Clouds**: Intersect the view ray with a flat cloud plane at height $H_c$.
   221→$$ \vec{P}_c = \vec{C} + \frac{H_c - C_z}{R_z} \vec{R} $$
   222→$$ D_{\text{cloud}} = \text{fBm}_{\text{cloud}}(P_{cx}, P_{cy}) $$
   223→$$ C_{\text{cloud}} = \text{smoothstep}(0.4, 0.6, D_{\text{cloud}}) \cdot \max(0, \vec{n}_{\text{cloud}} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) $$
   224→Clouds are blended over the sky gradient based on $D_{\text{cloud}}$.
   225→
   226→# 21. Color model
   227→
   228→Colors are defined in the **OKLab** perceptual color space to ensure that interpolations between biomes (e.g., green forest to brown desert) do not pass through muddy, desaturated grays, which happens in RGB or HSV.
   229→
   230→1. Define biome base colors in OKLab: $(L_k, a_k, b_k)$.
   231→2. Interpolate using biome weights $W_k$: $L = \sum W_k L_k$, etc.
   232→3. Add lighting: $L_{\text{final}} = L \cdot (L_{\text{ambient}} + L_{\text{diffuse}})$.
   233→4. Convert OKLab back to Linear sRGB, then apply gamma correction ($x^{1/2.2}$) for final output.
   234→
   235→# 22. Final compositing equation
   236→
   237→The final pixel color $C_{\text{pixel}}$ combines surface, lighting, and atmosphere:
   238→
   239→$$ C_{\text{surface}} = C_{\text{biome}} \cdot (L_{\text{ambient}} + L_{\text{diffuse}} \cdot L_{\text{shadow}}) + L_{\text{specular}} $$
   240→$$ C_{\text{pixel}} = \text{mix}(C_{\text{sky\_ray}}, C_{\text{surface}}, T_{\text{transmittance}}) + I_{\text{inscatter}} $$
   241→
   242→If the ray never intersects the terrain (looks at the sky), $C_{\text{pixel}} = C_{\text{sky\_ray}} + C_{\text{cloud}}$.
   243→
   244→# 23. Cursor/observer equations
   245→
   246→The observer's viewing direction is controlled by the mouse. We map mouse delta $\Delta M$ to spherical coordinates (yaw $\theta$, pitch $\phi$).
   247→
   248→$$ \theta_{\text{target}} = \theta_{\text{current}} + \Delta M_x \cdot S_{\text{sens}} $$
   249→$$ \phi_{\text{target}} = \text{clamp}(\phi_{\text{current}} + \Delta M_y \cdot S_{\text{sens}}, -\frac{\pi}{2} + \epsilon, \frac{\pi}{2} - \epsilon) $$
   250→
   251→The camera direction vector is:
   252→$$ \vec{D}_{\text{cam}} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\phi) \sin(\theta) \\ \sin(\phi) \\ \cos(\phi) \cos(\theta) \end{pmatrix} $$
   253→
   254→# 24. Camera smoothing equations
   255→
   256→To prevent jerky movement and simulate visual inertia, we use a critically damped spring system for position, and exponential smoothing for rotation.
   257→
   258→**Rotation (Exponential Smoothing)**:
   259→$$ \theta_{\text{current}} = \text{mix}(\theta_{\text{current}}, \theta_{\text{target}}, 1 - \exp(-k_{\text{rot}} \Delta t)) $$
   260→
   261→**Position (Spring-Damper)**:
   262→$$ \vec{a} = k_s (\vec{P}_{\text{target}} - \vec{P}_{\text{current}}) - k_d \vec{v} $$
   263→$$ \vec{v} = \vec{v} + \vec{a} \Delta t $$
   264→$$ \vec{P}_{\text{current}} = \vec{P}_{\text{current}} + \vec{v} \Delta t $$
   265→Where $k_d = 2 \sqrt{k_s}$ for critical damping (no oscillation).
   266→
   267→# 25. Infinite-world architecture
   268→
   269→The world is divided into conceptual chunks of size $2^{16}$ units.
   270→1. **CPU**: Maintains the global camera position $\vec{C}_{\text{global}}$ in 64-bit precision.
   271→2. **Chunking**: Calculates $\vec{C}_{\text{chunk}} = \text{floor}(\vec{C}_{\text{global}} / \text{ChunkSize}) \cdot \text{ChunkSize}$.
   272→3. **GPU Uniforms**: Passes $\vec{C}_{\text{chunk}}$ (as high-precision pair) and $\vec{C}_{\text{local}} = \vec{C}_{\text{global}} - \vec{C}_{\text{chunk}}$ (as 32-bit float).
   273→4. **Shader**: All raymarching and noise evaluations use $\vec{P}_{\text{local}} = \vec{C}_{\text{local}} + t \vec{R}$. This ensures the math never operates on numbers larger than $\approx 65536$, preserving 32-bit float precision and eliminating vertex/pixel jitter.
   274→
   275→# 26. GPU optimization strategy
   276→
   277→* **Adaptive Raymarching**: Step size $dt$ increases with distance $t$ ($dt = t \cdot 0.01$). Distant terrain requires less precision.
   278→* **Early Exit**: If the ray's $z$ component is positive and its current $z$ is above the maximum possible world height, terminate the ray and draw sky.
   279→* **LOD Noise**: High-frequency noise octaves are dropped when the ray intersection distance $t$ exceeds a threshold, saving ALU operations.
   280→* **Derivative Reuse**: The gradient $\nabla E$ calculated for the normal is reused for slope-based biome blending and river flow masks.
   281→* **Half-Resolution Atmosphere**: Fog and sky calculations can be done at half resolution and upsampled, as they lack high-frequency detail.
   282→
   283→# 27. Numerical precision strategy
   284→
   285→* **Global State**: `double` (Float64) for camera X, Y, Z.
   286→* **Local State**: `float` (Float32) for shader math.
   287→* **Hash Precision**: Integer `uint32` for hashing. Never use floating-point coordinates for hashing, as $1000000.1$ and $1000000.2$ may hash identically due to precision loss. Always cast to integer grid coordinates first: `uint ix = uint(floor(x))`.
   288→* **Continuity**: Ensure noise interpolation uses `fract(x)` and integer `floor(x)` to avoid seams at chunk boundaries.

   356→4. **Mountain**: Ridged noise yields $0.8$. Squared = $0.64$. $E_{\text{mount}} = 0.64 \cdot 1.0 = 0.64$.
   357→5. **Valley**: Worley edge distance $D_{\text{edge}} = 0.1$. Carve depth $= 0.2$.
   358→6. **Final Elevation**: $E_{\text{terrain}} = 0.475 + 0.64 - 0.2 = 0.915$.

   360→8. **Biome**: High weight for "Alpine Snow" and "Tundra".

   365→# 31. Pseudocode for the entire pipeline (CPU/Logic)
   366→
   367→```cpp
   368→struct Camera {
   369→    dvec3 pos_global;
   370→    vec3 pos_local;
   371→    vec3 velocity;
   372→    double yaw, pitch;
   373→    double target_yaw, target_pitch;
   374→};
   375→
   376→void update(Camera& cam, Input input, double dt) {
   377→    // 1. Mouse smoothing
   378→    cam.target_yaw += input.mouse_dx * SENSITIVITY;
   379→    cam.target_pitch += input.mouse_dy * SENSITIVITY;
   380→    cam.yaw = mix(cam.yaw, cam.target_yaw, 1.0 - exp(-10.0 * dt));
   381→    cam.pitch = clamp(mix(cam.pitch, cam.target_pitch, 1.0 - exp(-10.0 * dt)), -PI/2 + 0.1, PI/2 - 0.1);
   382→
   383→    // 2. Movement spring-damper
   384→    vec3 wish_dir = get_wish_direction(cam.yaw, input.keys);
   385→    vec3 accel = SPRING_K * (wish_dir * SPEED - cam.pos_local) - DAMPING_K * cam.velocity;
   386→    cam.velocity += accel * dt;
   387→    cam.pos_local += cam.velocity * dt;
   388→    
   389→    // 3. Chunking and precision rebasing
   390→    cam.pos_global += dvec3(cam.velocity * dt);
   391→    dvec3 chunk_origin = floor(cam.pos_global / CHUNK_SIZE) * CHUNK_SIZE;
   392→    cam.pos_local = vec3(cam.pos_global - chunk_origin);
   393→    
   394→    // 4. Upload to GPU
   395→    set_uniform("u_chunk_origin", chunk_origin);
   396→    set_uniform("u_cam_local", cam.pos_local);
   397→    set_uniform("u_yaw_pitch", vec2(cam.yaw, cam.pitch));
   398→}
   399→```

   404→#version 450
   405→layout(location = 0) out vec4 fragColor;
   406→uniform vec3 u_cam_local;
   407→uniform vec2 u_yaw_pitch;
   408→uniform float u_time;
   409→
   410→// [Include Noise, Hash, and fBm functions here]
   411→
   412→float get_elevation(vec2 p) {
   413→    vec2 warped = domain_warp(p);
   414→    float macro = fbm(warped * 0.001);
   415→    float mount = ridged_fbm(warped * 0.01) * smoothstep(0.0, 0.5, macro);
   416→    float valley = worley_edge(warped * 0.02) * 0.5;
   417→    return macro + mount - valley;
   418→}
   419→
   420→vec3 get_normal(vec2 p, float eps) {
   421→    float eL = get_elevation(p - vec2(eps, 0));
   422→    float eR = get_elevation(p + vec2(eps, 0));
   423→    float eD = get_elevation(p - vec2(0, eps));
   424→    float eU = get_elevation(p + vec2(0, eps));
   425→    return normalize(vec3(eL - eR, 2.0 * eps, eD - eU));
   426→}
   427→
   428→void main() {
   429→    vec2 uv = (gl_FragCoord.xy / resolution) * 2.0 - 1.0;
   430→    uv.x *= aspect_ratio;
   431→    
   432→    // Ray generation
   433→    vec3 ro = u_cam_local;
   434→    vec3 rd = get_ray_direction(uv, u_yaw_pitch, FOV);
   435→    
   436→    // Raymarching
   437→    float t = 0.0;
   438→    float t_max = 5000.0;
   439→    bool hit = false;
   440→    vec3 p;
   441→    
   442→    for (int i = 0; i < 128; i++) {
   443→        p = ro + rd * t;
   444→        float h = get_elevation(p.xz);
   445→        if (p.y < h) { hit = true; break; }
   446→        t += max(0.1, (p.y - h) * 0.5); // Adaptive step
   447→        if (t > t_max) break;
   448→    }
   449→    
   450→    vec3 col;
   451→    if (hit) {
   452→        vec3 n = get_normal(p.xz, t * 0.001);
   453→        float diffuse = max(0.0, dot(n, sun_dir));
   454→        vec3 base_col = get_biome_color(p.xz, get_elevation(p.xz));
   455→        col = base_col * (ambient + diffuse);
   456→        
   457→        // Atmosphere
   458→        float fog = 1.0 - exp(-t * fog_density);
   459→        col = mix(col, sky_color(rd), fog);
   460→    } else {
   461→        col = get_sky_and_clouds(ro, rd);
   462→    }
   463→    
   464→    fragColor = vec4(linear_to_srgb(col), 1.0);
   465→}

   483→# 34. Failure modes and how to fix them
   484→
   485→1. **Raymarching Leaks (Tunneling)**: The ray steps over a thin mountain peak.
   486→   * *Fix*: Use a relaxed marching factor ($\lambda_{\text{step}} < 1.0$) or implement a binary search refinement upon detecting a hit.
   487→2. **Aliasing / Moiré Patterns**: High-frequency noise shimmers when the camera moves.
   488→   * *Fix*: Scale the finite-difference $\epsilon$ for normals by the ray distance $t$. Drop noise octaves where the spatial frequency exceeds the Nyquist limit of the screen pixel.
   489→3. **Floating-Point Jitter**: Distant terrain vibrates violently.
   490→   * *Fix*: Strictly enforce the floating-origin (chunking) architecture. Never pass global coordinates to the GPU as 32-bit floats.
   491→4. **Muddy Biome Colors**: Interpolating RGB green and red yields brown.
   492→   * *Fix*: Strictly enforce OKLab color space interpolation for all biome blending.
   493→5. **Performance Bottlenecks**: Shadow raymarching halves the framerate.
   494→   * *Fix*: Limit shadow rays to a short distance (e.g., 50 units) and use a very low step count (4-8 steps). Distant shadows are hidden by fog anyway.

   496→# 35. Final master mathematical specification
   497→
   498→The complete visual output for a screen coordinate $(u,v)$ is defined hierarchically:
   499→
   500→$$
   501→\boxed{
   502→\begin{aligned}
   503→&\textbf{1. Projection \& Intersection:} \\
   504→&\vec{R}(u,v) = M_{\text{cam}}(\theta, \phi) \cdot \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \vec{P}(t) = \vec{C}_{\text{local}} + t \vec{R} \\
   505→&t^* = \min \{ t \mid P_z(t) \le E_{\text{terrain}}(P_x(t), P_y(t)) \} \\
   506→\\
   507→&\textbf{2. Environmental Fields (evaluated at } \vec{P}_{xz} = (P_x(t^*), P_y(t^*)): \\
   508→&E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) = E_{\text{macro}} + E_{\text{mount}}(W(x,y)) - E_{\text{valley}}(W(x,y)) \\
   509→&T(x,y) = T_{\text{base}} - \lambda_{\text{lat}} |Y_{\text{global}}| - \lambda_{\text{alt}} E_{\text{terrain}} \\
   510→&M(x,y) = M_{\text{base}} + N_{\text{moist}} + \lambda_{\text{ocean}} \exp(-D_{\text{ocean}}/\sigma) \\
   511→&W_k(x,y) = \frac{\exp(-\| (T,M,E) - C_k \|^2_{\Sigma})}{\sum_j \exp(-\| (T,M,E) - C_j \|^2_{\Sigma})} \\
   512→\\
   513→&\textbf{3. Shading \& Atmosphere:} \\
   514→&\vec{n} = \frac{(-\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}, 1)}{\|(-\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}, 1)\|}, \quad L_{\text{diff}} = \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) \cdot L_{\text{shadow}} \\
   515→&C_{\text{surface}} = \text{OKLab\_to\_sRGB}\left( \sum_k W_k C_{\text{biome},k} \right) \cdot (L_{\text{amb}} + L_{\text{diff}}) \\
   516→&\tau = \sigma_{\text{fog}} t^*, \quad T_{\text{trans}} = e^{-\tau}, \quad I_{\text{scatter}} = C_{\text{sky}}(\vec{R}) (1 - e^{-\tau}) \\
   517→\\
   518→&\textbf{4. Final Compositing:} \\
   519→&C_{\text{final}}(u,v) = C_{\text{surface}} \cdot T_{\text{trans}} + I_{\text{scatter}}
   520→\end{aligned}
   521→}
   522→$$
