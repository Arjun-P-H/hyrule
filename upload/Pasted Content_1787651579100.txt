# 1. Executive overview of the proposed mathematical world

The proposed system abandons conventional polygon-based rendering in favor of a **purely mathematical, continuous scalar field** evaluated in real-time via GPU raymarching. The world fundamentally exists as a 2D plane $(x,y) \in \mathbb{R}^2$, governed by a deterministic master function $W(x,y,\text{seed})$. This function outputs environmental properties (elevation, moisture, temperature, biome weights). 

To achieve the visual illusion of a 3D natural world, the 2D elevation field $E(x,y)$ is treated as a heightmap in a pseudo-3D space. A virtual camera casts mathematical rays through a 2D screen projection into this space, intersecting the continuous surface $z = E(x,y)$. Lighting, atmosphere, and depth are derived analytically from the gradients and intersection distances of this field. The result is an infinitely extensible, seamlessly coherent, and entirely asset-free natural environment where every visual pixel is a direct evaluation of underlying mathematics.

# 2. Fundamental mathematical assumptions

1. **Continuity**: The world is defined over $\mathbb{R}^2$. There are no discrete grids or voxels at the foundational level; discretization only occurs at the screen-sample (pixel) level.
2. **Determinism**: $W(x,y,\text{seed})$ is a pure function. It has no side effects and relies on no external state.
3. **Spatial Coherence**: The fields are Lipschitz continuous or smoothly differentiable almost everywhere, ensuring no visual tearing or infinite gradients.
4. **Multi-scale Self-Similarity**: The world exhibits fractal properties, where statistical characteristics repeat across spatial frequencies.
5. **Computational Locality**: The state at $(x,y)$ can be computed using only local mathematical operations and a bounded neighborhood, enabling massive parallelization on a GPU.

# 3. Coordinate system

The world utilizes a dual-coordinate architecture to balance infinite scale with numerical precision:
* **Global Coordinates $(X, Y)$**: Represents the absolute position in the infinite world. Stored as 64-bit floating-point (`double`) or a pair of 32-bit floats (double-float emulation) on the CPU to prevent precision loss at extreme distances.
* **Local Coordinates $(x, y)$**: Represents the position relative to the current camera chunk. $x = X - X_{\text{chunk}}$, $y = Y - Y_{\text{chunk}}$. Stored as 32-bit floating-point (`float`) in the GPU shader. All mathematical field evaluations use $(x,y)$ to ensure high precision and avoid floating-point jitter.

# 4. Seed and deterministic hashing

To ensure determinism without storing state, we use a 32-bit integer hash function based on the PCG/Murmur3 finalizer. This maps integer coordinates and a seed to a pseudo-random uniform distribution.

$$ H(x_i, y_i, s) = \text{finalize}(x_i \oplus (y_i \times 0x27D4EB2F) \oplus (s \times 0x9E3779B9)) $$

Where $\text{finalize}(h)$ applies bitwise shifts and multiplications to avalanche the bits:
$$ h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 16; \quad h \times= 0x85EBCA6B; \quad h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 13; \quad h \times= 0xC2B2AE35; \quad h \mathrel{\oplus}= h \gg 16 $$

To generate continuous noise, we evaluate $H$ at the integer lattice points $\lfloor x \rfloor, \lfloor y \rfloor$, convert to floats $U = H / 4294967295.0 \in [0,1]$, and interpolate using a quintic smoothstep $S(t) = 6t^5 - 15t^4 + 10t^3$ to ensure $C^2$ continuity (continuous second derivatives), eliminating visible grid artifacts.

# 5. Macro terrain equation

The macro terrain defines continents, oceans, and major tectonic plates. It uses low-frequency Fractal Brownian Motion (fBm).

$$ E_{\text{macro}}(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_{\text{macro}}-1} a_i N(f_i x, f_i y) $$

* **Variables**: $N$ is the base continuous noise function $\in [-1, 1]$. $f_i = f_0 \cdot L^i$ is the frequency (lacunarity $L \approx 2.0$). $a_i = P^i$ is the amplitude (persistence $P \approx 0.5$).
* **Range**: Approximately $[-1.5, 1.5]$, normalized to $[-1, 1]$.
* **Utility**: Creates broad, sweeping landmasses. Values $< 0$ conceptually represent ocean basins; values $> 0$ represent land.
* **Interaction**: Acts as a base mask and elevation offset for all meso and micro scales.

# 6. Mountain equation

Mountains require sharp ridges and asymmetric slopes, which standard fBm cannot produce. We use a **Ridged Multifractal** modulated by domain warping.

First, the ridge function:
$$ R(x,y) = 1.0 - |N(x,y)| $$
To sharpen the peaks, we square it: $R_{\text{sharp}} = R(x,y)^2$.

The mountain elevation field:
$$ E_{\text{mount}}(x,y) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_{\text{mount}}-1} a_i \left( 1.0 - |N(f_i x + W_x, f_i y + W_y)| \right)^2 \cdot M_{\text{macro}}(x,y) $$

* **Variables**: $W_x, W_y$ are domain warp offsets (see Sec 14). $M_{\text{macro}}$ is a mask derived from $E_{\text{macro}}$ ensuring mountains only form on land.
* **Range**: $[0, \text{MaxHeight}]$.
* **Utility**: The absolute value creates sharp discontinuities in the derivative (ridges). Squaring narrows the peaks. The mask prevents mountains from forming in the ocean.
* **Parameters**: Higher persistence ($P \approx 0.6$) creates more jagged, alpine peaks.

# 7. Valley equation

Valleys and canyons are carved using **Cellular (Worley) Noise**, which calculates the distance to the nearest randomly distributed seed point.

Let $F_1(x,y)$ be the distance to the nearest seed, and $F_2(x,y)$ be the distance to the second nearest. The distance to the cell boundary (the "edge") is:
$$ D_{\text{edge}}(x,y) = F_2(x,y) - F_1(x,y) $$

The valley carving function:
$$ E_{\text{valley}}(x,y) = C_{\text{depth}} \cdot \left( 1.0 - \text{smoothstep}(0, W_{\text{valley}}, D_{\text{edge}}(x,y)) \right)^2 $$

* **Variables**: $C_{\text{depth}}$ is maximum carving depth. $W_{\text{valley}}$ controls valley width.
* **Range**: $[0, C_{\text{depth}}]$.
* **Utility**: $D_{\text{edge}}$ is exactly $0$ at the boundaries between Voronoi cells. This creates a natural, branching network of low points that perfectly mimics fluvial erosion and drainage basins.
* **Interaction**: Subtracted from the combined macro and mountain elevation.

# 8. Water equation

Water is not an independent layer; it is a mathematical consequence of elevation and cellular boundaries.

**Oceans and Lakes**:
$$ W_{\text{body}}(x,y) = \text{smoothstep}(E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) - \epsilon, E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) + \epsilon, W_{\text{level}}) $$
Where $E_{\text{terrain}} = E_{\text{macro}} + E_{\text{mount}} - E_{\text{valley}}$.

**Rivers and Streams**:
Rivers flow along the Voronoi edges defined in the valley equation, but only where the terrain is above sea level and slope is sufficient.
$$ W_{\text{river}}(x,y) = \text{smoothstep}(W_{\text{river\_width}}, 0, D_{\text{edge}}(x,y)) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(W_{\text{level}}, W_{\text{level}} + 0.1, E_{\text{terrain}}) \cdot \|\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}\| $$

* **Utility**: This guarantees rivers naturally connect lakes, follow the steepest descent (via the gradient magnitude multiplier), and never flow uphill or exist in the ocean.

# 9. Climate equations

Climate is derived from global position, elevation, and noise, avoiding disconnected random maps.

**Temperature**:
$$ T(x,y) = T_{\text{base}} - \lambda_{\text{lat}} |Y_{\text{global}}| - \lambda_{\text{alt}} \max(0, E_{\text{terrain}}) + N_{\text{temp}}(x,y) $$
* **Variables**: $\lambda_{\text{lat}}$ is the latitude cooling rate. $\lambda_{\text{alt}}$ is the adiabatic lapse rate (altitude cooling). $N_{\text{temp}}$ is low-frequency noise for local climate anomalies.
* **Range**: $[-1, 1]$ (normalized, where $-1$ is freezing, $1$ is hot).

**Moisture**:
$$ M(x,y) = M_{\text{base}} + N_{\text{moist}}(x,y) + \lambda_{\text{ocean}} \exp(-D_{\text{ocean}}(x,y) / \sigma_{\text{moist}}) $$
* **Variables**: $D_{\text{ocean}}$ is the distance to the nearest ocean (approximated via the macro field). $\sigma_{\text{moist}}$ is the moisture decay distance.
* **Range**: $[0, 1]$.

# 10. Biome equation

Biomes are calculated using continuous radial basis functions (RBF) in the $(T, M, E)$ space, ensuring perfectly smooth transitions without hard thresholds.

Define $K$ biome centers $C_k = (T_k, M_k, E_k)$. The weight for biome $k$ is:
$$ w_k(x,y) = \exp\left( - \left( \frac{(T - T_k)^2}{\sigma_{T,k}^2} + \frac{(M - M_k)^2}{\sigma_{M,k}^2} + \frac{(E - E_k)^2}{\sigma_{E,k}^2} \right) \right) $$

Normalize the weights:
$$ W_k(x,y) = \frac{w_k(x,y)}{\sum_{j=1}^K w_j(x,y)} $$

* **Utility**: Any environmental property $P$ (color, vegetation density, roughness) is computed as $P(x,y) = \sum_{k=1}^K W_k(x,y) P_k$. This guarantees $C^\infty$ smooth blending between biomes.

# 11. Vegetation equation

Vegetation is a scalar density field $V(x,y)$ that modulates surface color and perturbs the surface normal to simulate grass/leaves without 3D models.

$$ V(x,y) = \left( \sum_{k=1}^K W_k(x,y) V_{\text{base},k} \right) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(0.2, 0.4, M(x,y)) \cdot \text{smoothstep}(0.8, 0.6, \|\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}\|) $$

* **Variables**: $V_{\text{base},k}$ is the max vegetation for biome $k$. The smoothsteps ensure plants only grow with sufficient moisture and on flat ground (not cliffs).
* **Visual Application**: $V(x,y)$ is used to mix rock/dirt colors with green colors, and added as high-frequency noise to the surface normal $\vec{n}$ to create a fuzzy, grass-like shading effect.

# 12. Geological anomaly equation

To create rare, unusual mathematical structures (e.g., spiral valleys, fractal craters), we use a low-frequency Voronoi grid to assign unique IDs to massive regions.

$$ \text{CellID} = \text{WorleyID}(X \cdot S_{\text{anom}}, Y \cdot S_{\text{anom}}) $$
$$ \text{AnomHash} = H(\text{CellID}_x, \text{CellID}_y, \text{seed}) / 4294967295.0 $$

If $\text{AnomHash} < P_{\text{anom}}$ (e.g., 0.005 for 0.5% rarity):
Calculate distance to cell center $D_c$. Apply a radial falloff $F = \text{smoothstep}(R_{\text{anom}}, 0, D_c)$.
Generate anomalous terrain $E_{\text{anom}}$ using non-standard math (e.g., $E_{\text{anom}} = \sin(D_c \cdot 10) \cdot \cos(\theta \cdot 5)$).
$$ E_{\text{final}} = \text{mix}(E_{\text{terrain}}, E_{\text{anom}}, F) $$

* **Utility**: Guarantees statistical rarity, absolute determinism, and spatial coherence without storing a database of landmarks.

# 13. Regional mathematical-rule equation

Large regions are governed by different terrain generation algorithms. We use a macro Voronoi grid (scale $S_{\text{region}} \approx 0.0001$).

Let $R_{\text{id}}$ be the region ID. We define different terrain functions:
* $f_0$: Standard fBm (Mountains)
* $f_1$: Trigonometric waves (Rolling dunes/hills)
* $f_2$: Quantized/Stepped noise (Mesas/Plateaus)

The blending weight between the current region and its neighbor is derived from the Worley edge distance $D_{\text{edge\_region}}$:
$$ B_{\text{region}} = \text{smoothstep}(0, W_{\text{blend}}, D_{\text{edge\_region}}) $$
The final terrain smoothly interpolates between the mathematical rules of adjacent regions.

# 14. Domain-warping system

Domain warping breaks the isotropic (directionally uniform) look of standard noise, creating organic, fluid-like geological folding.

$$ q(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \text{fBm}(x, y) \\ \text{fBm}(x + 5.2, y + 1.3) \end{pmatrix} $$
$$ r(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \text{fBm}(x + 4.0 q_x, y + 4.0 q_y) \\ \text{fBm}(x + 4.0 q_x + 1.7, y + 4.0 q_y + 9.2) \end{pmatrix} $$
$$ \begin{pmatrix} x' \\ y' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} + S_{\text{warp}} \cdot r(x,y) $$

* **Variables**: $S_{\text{warp}}$ is the warp strength.
* **Utility**: The nested fBm evaluations create chaotic, swirling distortions. When $E(x', y')$ is evaluated, it produces winding mountain ranges, meandering coastlines, and highly natural, non-repetitive structures.

# 15. Pseudo-3D projection model

I investigated five models. **Model E (Ray-like mathematical sampling / Heightfield Raymarching)** is the superior choice.

* **Model A (Orthographic)**: Lacks depth perception.
* **Model B (Perspective-warped 2D)**: Creates "treadmilling" artifacts and fails at steep angles.
* **Model C (Height-dependent)**: Complex to implement, breaks at horizons.
* **Model D (Layered depth)**: Looks like cardboard cutouts (parallax scrolling).
* **Model E (Raymarching)**: The 2D field $E(x,y)$ is treated as a surface $z = E(x,y)$ in 3D space. Rays are cast from the camera. This natively produces true perspective, occlusion, parallax, and shadows from a purely 2D mathematical field.

**Recommendation**: Model E. It perfectly fulfills the "2D world pretending to be 3D" requirement while providing genuine 3D visual fidelity.

# 16. Depth model

Depth is the distance $t$ along the view ray $\vec{R}$ from the camera origin $\vec{C}$ to the intersection point $\vec{P}$ where $P_z = E(P_x, P_y)$.

$$ \vec{P}(t) = \vec{C} + t \vec{R} $$
The raymarching loop solves for $t$ iteratively:
$$ t_{i+1} = t_i + \max(\Delta_{\text{min}}, (P_{z,i} - E(P_{x,i}, P_{y,i})) \cdot \lambda_{\text{step}}) $$
Where $\lambda_{\text{step}} \approx 0.5$ ensures we don't overstep the surface. Depth $t$ is used for fog, atmospheric scattering, and level-of-detail (LOD) noise cutoff.

# 17. Surface-normal model

The surface normal $\vec{n}$ is derived analytically from the elevation gradient $\nabla E$.

$$ \nabla E(x,y) = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{\partial E}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial E}{\partial y} \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} \frac{E(x+\epsilon, y) - E(x-\epsilon, y)}{2\epsilon} \\ \frac{E(x, y+\epsilon) - E(x, y-\epsilon)}{2\epsilon} \end{pmatrix} $$

The 3D normal vector is:
$$ \vec{n}(x,y) = \frac{(-\frac{\partial E}{\partial x}, -\frac{\partial E}{\partial y}, 1)}{\sqrt{(\frac{\partial E}{\partial x})^2 + (\frac{\partial E}{\partial y})^2 + 1}} $$
* **Optimization**: $\epsilon$ is scaled by the ray distance $t$ to maintain constant screen-space detail and avoid aliasing.

# 18. Lighting model

Lighting uses a Lambertian diffuse model with a Blinn-Phong specular highlight for water, plus shadow raymarching.

$$ L_{\text{diffuse}} = \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) $$
$$ L_{\text{specular}} = \left( \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{H}) \right)^{\alpha} \cdot W_{\text{body}} $$
Where $\vec{H} = \text{normalize}(\vec{L}_{\text{sun}} - \vec{R})$.

**Shadows**: To check if a point $\vec{P}$ is in shadow, march a secondary ray from $\vec{P}$ toward $\vec{L}_{\text{sun}}$. If $P_z(t) < E(P_x(t), P_y(t))$ at any point before reaching the sky, $L_{\text{shadow}} = 0$, else $1$.

# 19. Atmospheric model

Atmosphere is modeled using the Beer-Lambert law for light extinction and in-scattering.

$$ \tau(t) = \int_0^t \sigma(s) ds \approx \sigma_{\text{fog}} \cdot t $$
$$ T_{\text{transmittance}} = \exp(-\tau(t)) $$
$$ I_{\text{inscatter}} = C_{\text{sky}} \cdot (1 - \exp(-\tau(t))) $$

* **Variables**: $\sigma_{\text{fog}}$ is the scattering coefficient (density). $C_{\text{sky}}$ is the sky color at the ray's altitude.
* **Utility**: Distant objects smoothly fade into the sky color, providing massive depth cues and hiding the raymarching cutoff distance.

# 20. Sky model

The sky is a procedural gradient with raymarched volumetric clouds.

**Gradient**:
$$ C_{\text{sky}}(\vec{R}) = \text{mix}(C_{\text{horizon}}, C_{\text{zenith}}, \max(0, R_z)^{0.5}) $$

**Clouds**: Intersect the view ray with a flat cloud plane at height $H_c$.
$$ \vec{P}_c = \vec{C} + \frac{H_c - C_z}{R_z} \vec{R} $$
$$ D_{\text{cloud}} = \text{fBm}_{\text{cloud}}(P_{cx}, P_{cy}) $$
$$ C_{\text{cloud}} = \text{smoothstep}(0.4, 0.6, D_{\text{cloud}}) \cdot \max(0, \vec{n}_{\text{cloud}} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) $$
Clouds are blended over the sky gradient based on $D_{\text{cloud}}$.

# 21. Color model

Colors are defined in the **OKLab** perceptual color space to ensure that interpolations between biomes (e.g., green forest to brown desert) do not pass through muddy, desaturated grays, which happens in RGB or HSV.

1. Define biome base colors in OKLab: $(L_k, a_k, b_k)$.
2. Interpolate using biome weights $W_k$: $L = \sum W_k L_k$, etc.
3. Add lighting: $L_{\text{final}} = L \cdot (L_{\text{ambient}} + L_{\text{diffuse}})$.
4. Convert OKLab back to Linear sRGB, then apply gamma correction ($x^{1/2.2}$) for final output.

# 22. Final compositing equation

The final pixel color $C_{\text{pixel}}$ combines surface, lighting, and atmosphere:

$$ C_{\text{surface}} = C_{\text{biome}} \cdot (L_{\text{ambient}} + L_{\text{diffuse}} \cdot L_{\text{shadow}}) + L_{\text{specular}} $$
$$ C_{\text{pixel}} = \text{mix}(C_{\text{sky\_ray}}, C_{\text{surface}}, T_{\text{transmittance}}) + I_{\text{inscatter}} $$

If the ray never intersects the terrain (looks at the sky), $C_{\text{pixel}} = C_{\text{sky\_ray}} + C_{\text{cloud}}$.

# 23. Cursor/observer equations

The observer's viewing direction is controlled by the mouse. We map mouse delta $\Delta M$ to spherical coordinates (yaw $\theta$, pitch $\phi$).

$$ \theta_{\text{target}} = \theta_{\text{current}} + \Delta M_x \cdot S_{\text{sens}} $$
$$ \phi_{\text{target}} = \text{clamp}(\phi_{\text{current}} + \Delta M_y \cdot S_{\text{sens}}, -\frac{\pi}{2} + \epsilon, \frac{\pi}{2} - \epsilon) $$

The camera direction vector is:
$$ \vec{D}_{\text{cam}} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos(\phi) \sin(\theta) \\ \sin(\phi) \\ \cos(\phi) \cos(\theta) \end{pmatrix} $$

# 24. Camera smoothing equations

To prevent jerky movement and simulate visual inertia, we use a critically damped spring system for position, and exponential smoothing for rotation.

**Rotation (Exponential Smoothing)**:
$$ \theta_{\text{current}} = \text{mix}(\theta_{\text{current}}, \theta_{\text{target}}, 1 - \exp(-k_{\text{rot}} \Delta t)) $$

**Position (Spring-Damper)**:
$$ \vec{a} = k_s (\vec{P}_{\text{target}} - \vec{P}_{\text{current}}) - k_d \vec{v} $$
$$ \vec{v} = \vec{v} + \vec{a} \Delta t $$
$$ \vec{P}_{\text{current}} = \vec{P}_{\text{current}} + \vec{v} \Delta t $$
Where $k_d = 2 \sqrt{k_s}$ for critical damping (no oscillation).

# 25. Infinite-world architecture

The world is divided into conceptual chunks of size $2^{16}$ units.
1. **CPU**: Maintains the global camera position $\vec{C}_{\text{global}}$ in 64-bit precision.
2. **Chunking**: Calculates $\vec{C}_{\text{chunk}} = \text{floor}(\vec{C}_{\text{global}} / \text{ChunkSize}) \cdot \text{ChunkSize}$.
3. **GPU Uniforms**: Passes $\vec{C}_{\text{chunk}}$ (as high-precision pair) and $\vec{C}_{\text{local}} = \vec{C}_{\text{global}} - \vec{C}_{\text{chunk}}$ (as 32-bit float).
4. **Shader**: All raymarching and noise evaluations use $\vec{P}_{\text{local}} = \vec{C}_{\text{local}} + t \vec{R}$. This ensures the math never operates on numbers larger than $\approx 65536$, preserving 32-bit float precision and eliminating vertex/pixel jitter.

# 26. GPU optimization strategy

* **Adaptive Raymarching**: Step size $dt$ increases with distance $t$ ($dt = t \cdot 0.01$). Distant terrain requires less precision.
* **Early Exit**: If the ray's $z$ component is positive and its current $z$ is above the maximum possible world height, terminate the ray and draw sky.
* **LOD Noise**: High-frequency noise octaves are dropped when the ray intersection distance $t$ exceeds a threshold, saving ALU operations.
* **Derivative Reuse**: The gradient $\nabla E$ calculated for the normal is reused for slope-based biome blending and river flow masks.
* **Half-Resolution Atmosphere**: Fog and sky calculations can be done at half resolution and upsampled, as they lack high-frequency detail.

# 27. Numerical precision strategy

* **Global State**: `double` (Float64) for camera X, Y, Z.
* **Local State**: `float` (Float32) for shader math.
* **Hash Precision**: Integer `uint32` for hashing. Never use floating-point coordinates for hashing, as $1000000.1$ and $1000000.2$ may hash identically due to precision loss. Always cast to integer grid coordinates first: `uint ix = uint(floor(x))`.
* **Continuity**: Ensure noise interpolation uses `fract(x)` and integer `floor(x)` to avoid seams at chunk boundaries.

# 30. Fully worked numerical example at one world coordinate

Let $(X, Y) = (100.5, 200.3)$, $\text{seed} = 42$.
1. **Hash**: $H(100, 200, 42) \rightarrow \text{yields } 0.65$.
2. **Noise**: Interpolating lattice yields $N(100.5, 200.3) = 0.4$.
3. **Macro fBm**: $E_{\text{macro}} = 0.4 + 0.5(0.2) + 0.25(-0.1) = 0.475$ (Land).
4. **Mountain**: Ridged noise yields $0.8$. Squared = $0.64$. $E_{\text{mount}} = 0.64 \cdot 1.0 = 0.64$.
5. **Valley**: Worley edge distance $D_{\text{edge}} = 0.1$. Carve depth $= 0.2$.
6. **Final Elevation**: $E_{\text{terrain}} = 0.475 + 0.64 - 0.2 = 0.915$.
7. **Climate**: $T = 1.0 - 0.01(200) - 0.5(0.915) = -1.45 \rightarrow \text{clamped to } -1.0$ (Freezing). $M = 0.3$ (Dry).
8. **Biome**: High weight for "Alpine Snow" and "Tundra".
9. **Normal**: $\nabla E = (0.2, -0.1)$. $\vec{n} = (-0.2, 0.1, 1) / \text{length} \approx (-0.19, 0.09, 0.97)$.
10. **Lighting**: Sun $\vec{L} = (0.5, 0.8, 0.3)$. Diffuse $= \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{L}) = 0.28$.
11. **Color**: OKLab interpolation of Snow (White) and Rock (Gray). Modulated by lighting $0.28$. Final RGB $\approx (0.65, 0.68, 0.72)$.

# 31. Pseudocode for the entire pipeline (CPU/Logic)

```cpp
struct Camera {
    dvec3 pos_global;
    vec3 pos_local;
    vec3 velocity;
    double yaw, pitch;
    double target_yaw, target_pitch;
};

void update(Camera& cam, Input input, double dt) {
    // 1. Mouse smoothing
    cam.target_yaw += input.mouse_dx * SENSITIVITY;
    cam.target_pitch += input.mouse_dy * SENSITIVITY;
    cam.yaw = mix(cam.yaw, cam.target_yaw, 1.0 - exp(-10.0 * dt));
    cam.pitch = clamp(mix(cam.pitch, cam.target_pitch, 1.0 - exp(-10.0 * dt)), -PI/2 + 0.1, PI/2 - 0.1);

    // 2. Movement spring-damper
    vec3 wish_dir = get_wish_direction(cam.yaw, input.keys);
    vec3 accel = SPRING_K * (wish_dir * SPEED - cam.pos_local) - DAMPING_K * cam.velocity;
    cam.velocity += accel * dt;
    cam.pos_local += cam.velocity * dt;
    
    // 3. Chunking and precision rebasing
    cam.pos_global += dvec3(cam.velocity * dt);
    dvec3 chunk_origin = floor(cam.pos_global / CHUNK_SIZE) * CHUNK_SIZE;
    cam.pos_local = vec3(cam.pos_global - chunk_origin);
    
    // 4. Upload to GPU
    set_uniform("u_chunk_origin", chunk_origin);
    set_uniform("u_cam_local", cam.pos_local);
    set_uniform("u_yaw_pitch", vec2(cam.yaw, cam.pitch));
}
```

# 32. GPU shader-oriented pseudocode (Fragment Shader)

```glsl
#version 450
layout(location = 0) out vec4 fragColor;
uniform vec3 u_cam_local;
uniform vec2 u_yaw_pitch;
uniform float u_time;

// [Include Noise, Hash, and fBm functions here]

float get_elevation(vec2 p) {
    vec2 warped = domain_warp(p);
    float macro = fbm(warped * 0.001);
    float mount = ridged_fbm(warped * 0.01) * smoothstep(0.0, 0.5, macro);
    float valley = worley_edge(warped * 0.02) * 0.5;
    return macro + mount - valley;
}

vec3 get_normal(vec2 p, float eps) {
    float eL = get_elevation(p - vec2(eps, 0));
    float eR = get_elevation(p + vec2(eps, 0));
    float eD = get_elevation(p - vec2(0, eps));
    float eU = get_elevation(p + vec2(0, eps));
    return normalize(vec3(eL - eR, 2.0 * eps, eD - eU));
}

void main() {
    vec2 uv = (gl_FragCoord.xy / resolution) * 2.0 - 1.0;
    uv.x *= aspect_ratio;
    
    // Ray generation
    vec3 ro = u_cam_local;
    vec3 rd = get_ray_direction(uv, u_yaw_pitch, FOV);
    
    // Raymarching
    float t = 0.0;
    float t_max = 5000.0;
    bool hit = false;
    vec3 p;
    
    for (int i = 0; i < 128; i++) {
        p = ro + rd * t;
        float h = get_elevation(p.xz);
        if (p.y < h) { hit = true; break; }
        t += max(0.1, (p.y - h) * 0.5); // Adaptive step
        if (t > t_max) break;
    }
    
    vec3 col;
    if (hit) {
        vec3 n = get_normal(p.xz, t * 0.001);
        float diffuse = max(0.0, dot(n, sun_dir));
        vec3 base_col = get_biome_color(p.xz, get_elevation(p.xz));
        col = base_col * (ambient + diffuse);
        
        // Atmosphere
        float fog = 1.0 - exp(-t * fog_density);
        col = mix(col, sky_color(rd), fog);
    } else {
        col = get_sky_and_clouds(ro, rd);
    }
    
    fragColor = vec4(linear_to_srgb(col), 1.0);
}
```

# 34. Failure modes and how to fix them

1. **Raymarching Leaks (Tunneling)**: The ray steps over a thin mountain peak.
   * *Fix*: Use a relaxed marching factor ($\lambda_{\text{step}} < 1.0$) or implement a binary search refinement upon detecting a hit.
2. **Aliasing / Moiré Patterns**: High-frequency noise shimmers when the camera moves.
   * *Fix*: Scale the finite-difference $\epsilon$ for normals by the ray distance $t$. Drop noise octaves where the spatial frequency exceeds the Nyquist limit of the screen pixel.
3. **Floating-Point Jitter**: Distant terrain vibrates violently.
   * *Fix*: Strictly enforce the floating-origin (chunking) architecture. Never pass global coordinates to the GPU as 32-bit floats.
4. **Muddy Biome Colors**: Interpolating RGB green and red yields brown.
   * *Fix*: Strictly enforce OKLab color space interpolation for all biome blending.
5. **Performance Bottlenecks**: Shadow raymarching halves the framerate.
   * *Fix*: Limit shadow rays to a short distance (e.g., 50 units) and use a very low step count (4-8 steps). Distant shadows are hidden by fog anyway.

# 35. Final master mathematical specification

The complete visual output for a screen coordinate $(u,v)$ is defined hierarchically:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{1. Projection \& Intersection:} \\
&\vec{R}(u,v) = M_{\text{cam}}(\theta, \phi) \cdot \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \vec{P}(t) = \vec{C}_{\text{local}} + t \vec{R} \\
&t^* = \min \{ t \mid P_z(t) \le E_{\text{terrain}}(P_x(t), P_y(t)) \} \\
\\
&\textbf{2. Environmental Fields (evaluated at } \vec{P}_{xz} = (P_x(t^*), P_y(t^*)): \\
&E_{\text{terrain}}(x,y) = E_{\text{macro}} + E_{\text{mount}}(W(x,y)) - E_{\text{valley}}(W(x,y)) \\
&T(x,y) = T_{\text{base}} - \lambda_{\text{lat}} |Y_{\text{global}}| - \lambda_{\text{alt}} E_{\text{terrain}} \\
&M(x,y) = M_{\text{base}} + N_{\text{moist}} + \lambda_{\text{ocean}} \exp(-D_{\text{ocean}}/\sigma) \\
&W_k(x,y) = \frac{\exp(-\| (T,M,E) - C_k \|^2_{\Sigma})}{\sum_j \exp(-\| (T,M,E) - C_j \|^2_{\Sigma})} \\
\\
&\textbf{3. Shading \& Atmosphere:} \\
&\vec{n} = \frac{(-\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}, 1)}{\|(-\nabla E_{\text{terrain}}, 1)\|}, \quad L_{\text{diff}} = \max(0, \vec{n} \cdot \vec{L}_{\text{sun}}) \cdot L_{\text{shadow}} \\
&C_{\text{surface}} = \text{OKLab\_to\_sRGB}\left( \sum_k W_k C_{\text{biome},k} \right) \cdot (L_{\text{amb}} + L_{\text{diff}}) \\
&\tau = \sigma_{\text{fog}} t^*, \quad T_{\text{trans}} = e^{-\tau}, \quad I_{\text{scatter}} = C_{\text{sky}}(\vec{R}) (1 - e^{-\tau}) \\
\\
&\textbf{4. Final Compositing:} \\
&C_{\text{final}}(u,v) = C_{\text{surface}} \cdot T_{\text{trans}} + I_{\text{scatter}}
\end{aligned}
}
$$
