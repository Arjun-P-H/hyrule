# The Aelari and the Hollow Sky

The story behind the sky realm: who built the Eyries, and why each bridge, tower, cistern, garden and
lantern stands where it does. Every artefact the generator places traces back to a section here, and
§11 lists the rules that turn this story into code. The technical plan that builds it is
[EYRIES.md](EYRIES.md).

The people themselves (bodies, dress, voices) come later. This document only needs what shapes their
world: they **glide better than they fly**, they **perch and nest**, they **see far**, and they **live
by the wind and the water**.

---

## 1. The Hollow Sky

Above the cloud sea, from about 1.5 km to 3 km up, lies the **Hollow Sky**. The air is thin and bright
there, and the wind never stops. Islands float in it, from boulders a few metres across to mountains
upside down a kilometre wide. Their tops are meadow, forest and lake. Their undersides are bare rock
hanging in spikes, because the rock that holds them up hangs from them like roots.

The cloud sea below is the Hollow Sky's ocean. Every morning it breathes mist up the islands' flanks.
Every evening it takes back what falls from them.

## 2. Skystone, sunstone and why the islands float

**Skystone** (Aelari *vael*) is a pale blue crystal that falls upward: it is repelled by the deep earth
as a lodestone is repelled by its like. Veins of it run through the undersides of every island, and
where it is exposed it glows faintly at night. These are the blue veins on undersides and the
crystals in grottos.

Skystone does three things the Aelari build around:

1. **It lifts.** An island floats as long as its skystone outweighs its stone. Cut too much skystone
   from an island and it sinks; cut too much stone and it rises into the cold.
2. **It rings.** A large pure crystal hums when wind crosses it, like a struck bell. Nearby skystone
   hums with it.
3. **It holds.** Islands whose skystone hums together keep their places. Islands nobody sings to
   drift with the Gyre (§4.4). That is why lone isles wander, and why the Aelari gather islands into
   **Eyries**: clusters bound in formation by song and by bridges.

**Sunstone** (*ilu*) is skystone's amber cousin. It drinks daylight and gives it back as a warm glow
through the night. Every Aelari lantern is a sunstone in a glass cage, charged on a sunny ledge by
day. So the lanterns light themselves at dusk, and a city's lights come on as the sun sets.

## 3. History

### 3.1 The Rising (myth)
"When the Deep Fire woke, the earth shed its stones like feathers, and the stones flew." Long ago the
ground cracked and skystone-veined land tore free. It carried forests, lakes and the stone halls of
the **Ground-builders** up into the air. The Ground-builders were the older people whose ruins and
glowing shrines still stand on the land below. Some of their stones rode up with the islands, so
square-cut Ground-builder ruins also appear up here.

The Aelari's ancestors were cliff birds of the high mountains. They followed the rising islands and
nested in their undersides.

### 3.2 The First Roosts
For a thousand years they lived in caves and ledges under the islands, and in the roots of the
oldest trees. They climbed up to the tops only for fruit and water. They learned to weave nests, to
read the wind and to glide from island to island. Their oldest homes, the grottos and cliff-nests,
are still there under the great isles.

### 3.3 The Binding
A singer named **Ilyth of the Long Breath** found that skystone answers song. A chorus singing to a
great crystal could steady the islands around it. Around that crystal the first **Halo** was raised:
a ring of stone so the wind itself would keep singing when the singers slept. Within a generation the
first Eyrie hung still in the sky.

The first bridges followed. Every bridge was a vow between two broods: *no isle shall drift alone*.
With the bridges came the **Accord of Breath** (§5), the law every Eyrie still keeps.

### 3.4 The High Eyries
The golden age, called the Age of Spires:
- **Highcrown** grew into the first city, and **Mirrorwell** became the keeper of the water law.
- A chain of crystal beacons, the **Lanternpeaks**, was lit across the Hollow Sky. Each sends a
  thread of light upward that skyships steer by, and that keeps distant Eyries humming in tune.
- The Windwrights learned to float ships on skystone keels under sail.
- Aqueducts, bells, glass and great domes date from this age.

### 3.5 The Long Gale
About three hundred years ago the forge-lords of **Cinderforge** cut skystone from living islands to
build warships. The song of the Eyries went out of tune. A storm opened over the north and never
closed: the **Gyre's Eye**, a vortex of cloud that still turns above **Rimewatch**.

The storm tore the Eyrie of **Hushfall** apart. Its islands tilted and scattered, its bridges fell,
its spires snapped. Tens of thousands took wing and never found it again.

### 3.6 The Mending (the present)
The survivors renewed the Accord and made it stricter:
- No skystone is cut from a living island. It is gathered only from debris and fallen fragments.
- No forge burns anything but the earth's own fire.
- Every drop of water is counted.
- Hushfall is left as it fell: a memorial and a sanctuary for wild birds.
- Rimewatch keeps watch on the Gyre's Eye.

New bridges are built from stone salvaged from debris. The civilization the player finds is living
and renewing, with old wounds in plain sight.

## 4. The people

### 4.1 Wings and height
The Aelari glide on broad wings and fly in short bursts. Climbing is hard work for them, and gliding
down is easy. That shapes everything they build:
- **They launch from heights and land on ledges.** Every dwelling is a tower with a perch-ledge at
  each floor and a launch loft at the top.
- **The flightless need stairs.** Hatchlings cannot fly until their First Flight at about seven.
  Elders, the injured, guests and anyone carrying a load also walk. So every tower has a spiral ramp
  or stair, and every Eyrie has bridges.
- **Storms ground everyone.** When the Gyre blows hard, flying between islands kills, so bridges
  are the storm roads.
- **Loads go by water, rope and ship**, never on the wing: aqueducts, lifts and skyships.

### 4.2 Nests, food and the senses
- **They sleep in nests:** round woven bowls lined with down, raised on platforms high in their
  towers.
- **They eat fruit, seed, fish and nectar.** Orchards (the blossom trees), seed terraces, fishponds
  in the lagoons and flower gardens all exist to feed an Eyrie.
- **They see far.** Long sightlines, beacons, high windows and look-out spires everywhere.
- **They love light.** Glass, lanterns, crystal and coloured windows.
- **They feel the wind.** Wind chimes, bells and banners are how they read it. A banner's stream
  tells a flier where the gust is.

### 4.3 Broods, Houses and guilds
- **Broods** (extended families) share a tower-house, called a **roost**.
- **Seven plumage Houses** carry heraldic colours, the banners on towers and plazas. Highcrown's
  colours are night-blue and gold.
- **Guilds** keep the Accord and the works:
  - *Windwrights* build;
  - *Mistwardens* keep the water;
  - *Stonesingers* keep the skystone in tune;
  - *Lamplighters* charge and hang the sunstones;
  - *Shipwrights* build the skyships;
  - *Orchard-keepers* tend the trees;
  - *Cord-keepers* keep the archives, knotted cords and carved runes;
  - *Wardens* guard bridges and gates.

Each Eyrie is ruled by its **Moot of Wings**, a council that meets in a round hall. Highcrown hosts
the Grand Moot of all Eyries.

### 4.4 Belief
The **Gyre** is the great wind that circles the world. Souls ride it, and it carries the Aelari's
dead. They are given to the wind from the **Silent Spires**, and their feathers become clouds.

The **Wellspring** is the spirit of water, honoured at every fountain. Ancestors stand in stone as
**Warden statues**, wings spread, at bridge ends and gates. Sacred places are round, like the Gyre:
the Halo, the rotunda and the plaza.

### 4.5 Arts
- **Windsong.** The Halo and the spires are instruments.
- **Glass and lanterns.**
- **Weaving:** banners, sails, nests and awnings.
- **Stone carving:** feather motifs, scale tiles and spiral columns.
- **Floors:** radial mosaics like an opened wing.
- **Bells and chimes.**

## 5. The Accord of Breath

*Take from the sky only what returns to it.* An Eyrie keeps five cycles, and each one is visible in
its buildings.

| Cycle | How it runs | What you see |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Water** | Mist is combed from the cloud sea on low islands. Cisterns store it, aqueducts carry it, terraces and fountains use it, and the overflow falls back to the clouds as **Veils** (waterfalls). Nothing is pumped higher than the wind can lift it. | cloud-combs, wind pumps, cisterns, aqueducts on bridges, fountains, water stairs, falls from cisterns and lagoons |
| **Food** | Orchards (blossom in spring, fruit in summer), seed terraces, fishponds, flowers for nectar; compost returns to the soil | blossom groves round every roost, terraced gardens, lotus ponds with koi, greenhouses on cold isles |
| **Light and heat** | Sunstone lanterns charge by day and glow by night; glass domes keep warm crops; forges use only volcanic heat | lanterns on every path, glowing windows at dusk, glass domes, lava-lit forges |
| **Stone** | Building stone is salvaged from debris; skystone is gathered only from fallen fragments, and a tithe of it is kept at the Halo | pale salvaged limestone, glowing blue keystones, debris quarries with cranes, no scars on living isles |
| **Life** | Wild isles are left alone as sanctuaries; a tree is planted for every hatchling | untouched isles full of birds, the Elder Bough, avenues of trees |

## 6. Architecture: the language of building

Each principle names its reason and the rule the generator follows.

1. **Height is home.** Aelari live high, for safety, view and launch. Dwellings are tower-roosts of 3–8
   floors, never low houses. Ground floors hold stores and the flightless.
2. **Every floor has a ledge.** Perch-ledges (balconies) ring towers at floor heights. Doors open
   onto air as often as onto streets.
3. **Open to the wind.** Round towers shed gusts. Walls are pierced by tall pointed arches so the wind
   passes through instead of pushing. Arcades, colonnades and open belfries are everywhere.
4. **Spires are instruments.** Every spire carries a bell or wind-harp, a lightning rod and a
   lantern. A tall spire is a landing beacon seen from kilometres away. Spires end in finials;
   belfries open under them.
5. **Roofs are feathers.** Tiles are cut like scales and laid like feathers, steep to shed rain and
   snow. Colours: slate blue, verdigris copper, terracotta and gold.
6. **Pale stone from fallen isles.** Walls are salvaged limestone. Every arch has a **skystone
   keystone** that glows faintly blue at night.
7. **Bridges are vows.** Islands are joined by multi-arch viaducts. Their piers cannot stand on
   ground, so they **hang**: each pier tapers into a **plumb-stone**, a skystone weight shaped like an
   inverted spire that keeps the bridge from swaying. Warden statues and lanterns stand at both ends.
   Many bridges carry an aqueduct beside the walk.
8. **Water is sacred and seen.** It runs in open channels on bridges, sings in fountains on every
   plaza, steps down water stairs, and leaves the Eyrie in Veils. A fall is the Eyrie breathing out.
9. **Light at night.** Sunstone lanterns line paths, hang from trees and arches, and stand at every
   bridge end. Windows glow.
10. **Gardens everywhere.** Orchards and flowering trees, for food and for joy: avenues along
    streets, groves round roosts, wisteria on palace walls.
11. **Banners read the wind.** Each roost flies its brood's banner, and plazas fly the Houses'.
12. **Circles are sacred.** Halls of council and worship are round, as are plazas and the Halo.
13. **Nothing wasted.** Ruins are left where they fell when they are sacred (Hushfall), and quarried
    for stone when they are debris.

## 7. The Eyries

An Eyrie is a bound cluster of islands. Each belongs to one of eleven **classes**, has 0–2
**features** borrowed from compatible classes, and draws its own size, height, arrangement, palette and
architecture from its cell's hash. So no two are alike, and each is rare: an Eyrie is a destination,
not scenery. Between them lies open sky with a few wild isles and wayshrines.

Each Eyrie also gets a proper name (e.g. *Sael Vhara, the Crown of Wings*).

### 7.1 Crown: the capital Eyries (*e.g. Sael Vhara, the Crown of Wings*)
**Purpose.** The seat of a Moot and its Houses: worship, council, archives, markets and the great
roosts.

**Layout.** A broad central **Crown isle** (600–900 m), domed gently, is surrounded by 4–7 **ward
isles**, each a little lower. Each ward has one task: market, archive, cisterns, gardens, docks or
roosts. Vow-bridges (viaducts with hanging piers) join every ward to the Crown. A ring of small
**perch isles** with watchtowers sits outside them.

**Signature artefacts:**
- **The Gyre Cathedral** on a podium at the summit: a nave and transepts, a crossing tower with an
  **oculus**, and 1–5 needle spires. The congregation perches in galleries at three heights; the wind
  altar stands under the oculus.
- **The Moot Rotunda**: round, domed, with tiered perches.
- **Palace towers** with wisteria terraces.
- **Rings of roosts** along radial streets.
- **Wind walls** with turrets on the windward rim.
- **A grand stair** down to the **Fountain Plaza**, with its radial mosaic.
- **The cistern lagoon**, which spills over the rim as the Great Veil.

**Palette.** White limestone, slate-blue or verdigris roofs, gold finials, night-blue and gold
banners.

**Life.** Swift flocks round the spires, hawks above, koi in the lagoon, blossom avenues.

### 7.2 Mirrorwell: the water temple (*Ilun Sa, the Mirror of the Accord*)
**Purpose.** Keeper of the water law. Every Eyrie's Mistwardens train here. The Rotunda of the Accord
holds the **Measure**, a stone basin whose level the whole Hollow Sky trusts.

**Layout.** A great **bowl isle** (450–700 m) is mostly a turquoise **lagoon**. In its middle a round
plaza stands on piers with the **Rotunda of the Accord** on it, reached by a causeway of arches.
Terraces of colonnades and pavilions ring the shore. 5–9 **well isles** float higher round the bowl.
Their streams pour into the lagoon as the **Veils**: falls that land in it, not in the clouds. The
lagoon itself spills over the rim through one carved notch, the Great Veil.

**Signature artefacts.** The Rotunda and plaza with a radial mosaic, the arched causeway, lotus beds,
fish-ponds, boats, measuring steps on the shore, fountains on the terraces.

**Palette.** White marble, turquoise domes, bronze, sea-green banners.

**Life.** Koi under lotus and lily pads, herons, lantern-flies over the water at dusk.

### 7.3 Ringhold: the Halo sanctum (*Oru Rhen, the Singing Ring*)
**Purpose.** Where the Binding began. The **Great Halo** (150–300 m across) stands upright on its isle
and sings in the wind. The **Stonesingers** live in the monastery below and keep every Eyrie's song
in tune. Pilgrims come up the spiral.

**Layout.** A tall **Halo isle** with the ring standing in a cleft across it. A **spiral of pilgrim
isles** (5–9) climbs round it, joined by stair-bridges. **Runestones**, floating stones carved with
glowing blue runes, circle the ring slowly.

**Signature artefacts.** The Halo (carved bands, buttresses at its feet), the cloister and cells,
the singing courts, the pilgrim stairs, Warden statues.

**Palette.** Weathered grey-white stone, moss, blue rune-glow.

**Life.** The wind through the ring (a hum at night), moss gardens, white birds.

### 7.4 Lanternpeak: the beacon (*Vaelspire*)
**Purpose.** A link in the Lattice. A great skystone crystal on a mountain-isle sends the
**Thread**, a beam of pale light, up into the sky. Skyships steer by it, and it keeps distant Eyries in
tune. Astronomers and lamp-wardens live here.

**Layout.** A **peak isle** (300–500 m) rises to a snowy summit crowned by the **Vaelspire**, a
cluster of crystal 60–120 m tall. Switchback stairs climb the peak, and observatory domes stand on its
shoulders. Small crystal isles float close by. Its underside is laced with glowing veins.

**Signature artefacts.** The Vaelspire and the Thread, observatories (brass domes with slits), the
lamp-wardens' tower, the star-court with its orrery.

**Palette.** Pale stone, violet-blue crystal, brass.

**Life.** High cold air, few trees, swifts circling the beam at night.

### 7.5 Petalfall: the orchard terraces (*Thessa Vel, the Falling Petal*)
**Purpose.** Food for many Eyries: blossom orchards (sky plum, pearl pear, amber quince), tea and
seed terraces, honey-flowers.

**Layout.** A **chain** of 5–9 isles curving down like a stair, each 10–40 m below the last. The
**Stair of Waters**, a river, rises in a spring-lake on the top isle and runs down the whole chain:
each isle's stream pours into the next isle's pool.

**Signature artefacts.**
- **Wind-houses**: tiered towers with upswept eaves, so the wind slips over them.
- **Tea pavilions** and **vermilion gate-arches**.
- **Arched wooden bridges** and **rope bridges**.
- **Paper lanterns** strung between trees.

**Palette.** Dark timber, vermilion lacquer, grey-green tiles, white plaster.

**Life.** Petals on the wind and on every pool, petalwing butterflies, koi, red ember maples among the
pink.

### 7.6 Elder Bough: the world tree (*Ess Aeli, the First Nest*)
**Purpose.** The tree under whose roots the First Roosts were made. Every hatchling of the Hollow Sky
makes its First Flight here, and it is the nursery of all Eyries.

**Layout.** A central **root isle** holds a **colossal tree**, 150–250 m tall with a crown over 200 m
wide. Its roots hang below the isle and reach across to 3–5 neighbouring isles, binding them as
bridges of living wood.

**Signature artefacts.** Nest-towns on platforms in the branches, ramps spiralling up the trunk, the
**Leap** (the First Flight platform), lanterns hung in the boughs, nursery nests.

**Palette.** Living wood, woven reeds, moss, warm lantern light.

**Life.** Thousands of birds, falling leaves, fireflies in the roots.

### 7.7 Cinderforge: the forge isles (*Kesh Thra, the Ember Anvil*)
**Purpose.** Metal, glass and skyships, and penance. Since the Long Gale its forges burn only the
islands' own volcanic heat, and its stonesingers tune salvaged skystone into ship keels.

**Layout.** A tight cluster of jagged **basalt isles** with a **lava vent** on the main one. Glowing
**lava-falls** pour off one rim. **Skyship yards** sit on the leeward isles.

**Signature artefacts.** Forges with tall chimneys and smoke, the dark citadel of the old forge-lords
(now the Hall of Penance), slipways with ships under construction, iron bridges and chains.

**Palette.** Black basalt, iron, bronze, ember orange.

**Life.** Smoke, sparks, red ember maples on the warm soil, crows.

### 7.8 Rimewatch: the frost citadel (*Ysh Varra, the Ice Watch*)
**Purpose.** Watching the **Gyre's Eye**, the storm that opened in the Long Gale and never closed. The
storm-wardens warn every Eyrie of its moods.

**Layout.** High **ice isles** (tops 2.6–2.9 km) under snow, with a fortress of heavy walls and round
towers. The vortex turns in the clouds above.

**Signature artefacts.** Watchtowers with braziers, ice cisterns, signal mirrors, the long stair, wind
walls.

**Palette.** Blue-grey stone, ice, silver roofs, white banners.

**Life.** Rime pines, frozen lakes, snow-hawks.

### 7.9 Hushfall: the Fallen Eyrie (*memorial and sanctuary*)
**Purpose.** What the Long Gale left. Nobody lives here now. Pilgrims come to remember, and wild
birds, butterflies and blossom have taken it back.

**Layout.** A broken Crown:
- isles tilted and scattered, with many debris;
- bridges fallen, leaving lone piers hanging in the air;
- spires snapped and roofs fallen in;
- arches standing alone.

**Signature artefacts.** Ruined cathedral shells, broken towers, fallen columns, overgrown plazas,
and lone Warden statues still guarding bridges that are gone.

**Palette.** Weathered stone, moss, wild pink and white blossom.

**Life.** The wildest place in the sky: flocks, butterflies and fireflies.

### 7.10 Galeharbour: the trade port (*Vey Dorran, the Windmarket*)
**Purpose.** Where skyships from every Eyrie dock and trade.

**Layout.** Isles in a **ring round an open anchorage** where ships hover at mooring masts.
Long **docks** jut from the rims.

**Signature artefacts.** Cranes, warehouses, market terraces with striped awnings and stalls,
lighthouses, banners of every House, moored skyships.

**Palette.** Sandstone, timber, colourful cloth.

**Life.** Ships coming and going along the trade routes, gulls, market bustle.

### 7.11 Mistwells: the combing isles (*Sa Rhen, the Breath Wells*)
**Purpose.** Water for the cities. Low isles hang in the cloud deck where the mist is thickest.

**Layout.** Low **isles** (tops 1.6–1.9 km) with **cloud-combs**: huge lattice nets on masts that
gather mist into gutters. Cisterns and **wind-pump towers** with turning sails stand beside them, and
**water-barge docks** load the skyships that carry water up to the cities.

**Signature artefacts.** Cloud-combs, wind-wheels, cisterns, barge docks.

**Palette.** Weathered timber, grey stone, canvas.

**Life.** Mistwood forest, moss, ferns, herons.

### 7.12 Between the Eyries
Open sky, with a few:
- **wild isles**: sanctuaries where nothing is built;
- **wayshrines**: a single lantern-stone where ships take their bearing;
- **hermit roosts**: one tower and one tree;
- **drifting Ground-builder ruins**.

## 9. The living sky

### 9.1 Flora
- **Orchard trees** (food first, beauty second):
  - sky plum: pink blossom;
  - pearl pear: white;
  - amber quince: golden;
  - ember maple: red, fond of warm soil.
- **Wisteria** hangs purple over palace terraces and arbours.
- **Mistwood oaks and tree-ferns** grow on the low wet isles; **rime pines** on the high cold ones.
- **Lotus and lily pads** cover lagoons and pools; **waterweed** sways underwater; mossy **stones**
  line the beds.
- **Wildflowers:** pink cosmos, white daisies and violet bellflowers in every meadow and crack.
- The **Elder Bough**.

### 9.2 Fauna
- **Cloud-swifts:** dark flocks that wheel round spires at dawn and dusk and nest under eaves.
- **Gyre-hawks:** great raptors soaring in wide circles above Eyries on the thermals.
- **Sky-herons:** white birds of lagoons and Mistwells.
- **Sky-koi:** orange, white and gold fish of the lagoons and pools.
- **Petalwings:** butterflies of the orchards.
- **Lantern-flies:** fireflies that rise from water and meadow at dusk.
- **Crows** at Cinderforge, **gulls** at Galeharbour.

### 9.3 How it holds together
- Petals fall into the pools and the koi eat them. Koi waste feeds the lotus, and lotus shades the
  koi.
- Petalwings pollinate the orchards and the swifts eat the gnats.
- Hawks keep the swift flocks moving.
- The falls' mist waters the mistwood, and the mistwood holds the mist that the combs gather.
- The Elder Bough feeds thousands of birds, whose droppings feed the isles' soil.

## 11. Rules for the generator

The rules that turn this story into code. Each section number points back to the reason.

1. **Eyries are rare and whole.** At most one per 8 km cell, and fewer than today's archipelagos.
   Each has one class (§7), 0–2 features, its own name, and parameters from its cell's hash. Lone
   isles are wild isles, wayshrines, hermit roosts or ruins (§7.12).
2. **Islands follow the class.** The class fixes the arrangement:
   - radial wards (Crown);
   - a bowl and its well isles (Mirrorwell);
   - a helix round the ring (Ringhold);
   - a peak with crystal isles (Lanternpeak);
   - a descending chain (Petalfall);
   - a tree-bound cluster (Elder Bough);
   - tight basalt shards (Cinderforge);
   - high ice (Rimewatch);
   - a broken Crown (Hushfall);
   - a harbour ring (Galeharbour);
   - low cloud-deck isles (Mistwells).

   The class also fixes the underside: spikes, crystal veins (§2), roots (§7.6), basalt (§7.7) or
   ice (§7.8).
3. **Homes are towers** (§6.1–6.4): roosts of 3–8 floors with ledges, spires with finials, banners.
4. **Every bridge has hanging piers** with plumb-stones, and Wardens and lanterns at its ends
   (§6.7). Bridges join isles whose heights differ by less than the slope a stair can take; others
   are reached by ship or wing.
5. **Water has a source and a way out** (§5): cistern → aqueduct → fountain or terrace → fall. A fall
   either lands in a pool on an isle below (Petalfall, Mirrorwell) or turns to mist above the cloud
   sea.
6. **Gardens feed the Eyrie** (§5, §9.1): orchard groves round roosts and along streets; lotus and
   koi in every pool.
7. **Lanterns light every path, bridge end, gate and dock** (§6.9). They wake at dusk; windows glow
   from dusk to dawn.
8. **Sacred places are round** (§6.12): plazas, rotundas and the Halo.
9. **Nothing grows on paving or inside walls.** Grass and trees keep off streets, plazas and
   footprints.
10. **Every building can be entered if its kind has an inside** (§8), and its inside follows its
    purpose.
11. **Life moves** (§9.2, §10):
    - swifts round spires, hawks above, koi in pools, petalwings in orchards;
    - lantern-flies at dusk, petals on the wind;
    - skyships on routes between Eyries;
    - banners in the wind, wind-wheels turning;
    - the Thread, the Halo's runes, the Gyre's Eye.
12. **The Long Gale left marks** (§3.5): Hushfall's ruins, the Gyre's Eye over Rimewatch, and the
    occasional fallen tower in a living Eyrie, kept as it fell.
